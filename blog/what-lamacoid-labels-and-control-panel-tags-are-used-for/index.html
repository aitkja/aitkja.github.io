What Are Lamacoid Labels?

Lamacoid labels (also spelled lamicoid) are multi-layer plastic identification plates. Laser engraving removes the top colour layer so contrasting lettering shows through. The result is a durable legend plate or panel tag that stays readable on doors, operator stations, and equipment after install. Buyers searching “what are lamacoid labels” or “what are lamacoid tags” are usually deciding whether engraved plastic panel stock fits their control cabinet or electrical schedule.

  • Machine labels, asset-style markers, and equipment panel markers

Legend Plates vs Broader Industrial Tags

Legend plates and control-panel labeling focus on operator-facing text: START/STOP, mode selectors, lamp functions, and matching text across a panel schedule. Valve tags and process identifiers use the same stock class when a plant needs engraved plastic markers that survive handling. Machine ID plates and OEM data plates are a different job shape—those buyers should use phenolic / industrial nameplates rather than lamacoid panel labels.

Phenolic Labels as a Trade Synonym

In electrical and panel trade language, “phenolic labels” or “phenolic labels for electrical panels” often means the same engraved multi-layer plastic tags people call lamacoid. For panel labels, legend plates and breaker tags, both words lead to the same service: lamacoid labels in London, Ontario. Lamacoid FAQ: Materials, Sizes, Colours & Uses

What is a lamacoid label, and what is it made of?

A lamacoid (also spelled lamicoid) is an engraved label, tag or plate cut from multi-layer engraving plastic: a thin colour cap bonded over a contrasting core. Engraving removes the cap so the letters show in the core colour, for example black letters on a white label. The word is a common Canadian trade term rather than one specific product, and modern lamacoid stock is usually a laminated acrylic or similar engraving plastic. Older and specification-driven stock may be phenolic laminate.

Are phenolic labels the same as lamacoid?

In electrical-trade conversation they are usually treated as the same thing: an engraved two-colour plastic panel label. Strictly speaking, phenolic is a resin-based laminate and many current lamacoid stocks are acrylic-based, so if a specification calls for phenolic material, say so on the quote. For panel labels see lamacoid labels; for machine and equipment plates see phenolic nameplates.

What is the difference between lamacoid and acrylic?

Lamacoid is built for identification: two contrasting layers, so engraved text reads clearly at a glance on a panel, valve or machine. A solid or clear acrylic sheet is one colour, so engraving gives a frosted or tone-on-tone mark that suits display and business signs better than small ID labels. For signs and display pieces, see custom signage.

Are there standard lamacoid sizes, thicknesses and colours?

There is no single standard size; the size usually comes from the device, the panel or the project specification. Common thicknesses are about 1/16" (1.6 mm) for labels and legends and about 1/8" (3.2 mm) for stiffer plates. Common colour pairs include white with black letters, black with white, red with white for emergency or warning text, and yellow with black. Tell us the colour pair your spec calls for and we will confirm the stock. Pieces must fit our 20" × 28" bed.

Is there a lamacoid template I should use?

You do not need a special template. A spreadsheet with one row per label (text, size, colour and quantity) is the easiest way to send a set. We work in LightBurn, so vector artwork or LightBurn files can be used as-is.

How should an electrical panel be labelled?

Each breaker, fuse and switch should be marked clearly enough to show what it controls, using specific descriptions such as the room or equipment rather than just “lights” or “plugs”. In Ontario, the 2024 Electrical Safety Code (in force since May 1, 2025) also requires the marking to be updated when circuits at an existing panel are added, removed or changed. This is general information, not code advice; your electrical contractor and the Electrical Safety Authority decide what is required. We engrave the panel and breaker labels to the wording you provide.

What are valve tags used for?

Valve tags identify each valve by number and service so operators and maintenance crews can match it to the valve chart, find the right valve quickly during shutdowns, and keep records consistent. They usually hang from the valve on a chain or S-hook. We engrave plastic valve tags and can black-mark metal tags.

What information is required on a lockout tag?

Your employer's lockout program sets the requirement, usually guided by Ontario workplace safety regulations and CSA Z460. Lockout tags commonly identify the worker who applied the lock and how to reach them, and often the department, date and reason. Permanent details such as a name or lock number suit engraving, while changing details usually go on a write-on tag. See safety lock engraving for numbered lockout sets.

Can you laser engrave phenolic?

Yes. We engrave phenolic and lamacoid engraving plastics, along with acrylic, wood and black marking on metal. If your drawings call for a specific phenolic grade, include it on the quote so we can confirm the stock.

What about arc flash labels?

Arc flash label values come from an engineering arc flash study, which in Canada is typically done to CSA Z462. We can engrave durable labels with the information your engineer provides, but we do not perform or interpret those studies.
